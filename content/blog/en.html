Once you decide to have surgery, the same question usually comes up: "What should I do now?" Good preparation shapes both the safety of the operation and the quality of your result. The reassuring part is that this preparation is simpler than most people expect, as long as you take a few steps on time.

Here is a week by week guide to preparing for aesthetic surgery. Your surgeon's instructions for your specific case always come first, but the general framework is similar for almost every procedure.

Why does preparation before aesthetic surgery matter so much?

Preparation lowers the risk of complications and speeds up healing. Smoking, certain medications and poor nutrition affect bleeding, swelling and how quickly your wounds close. Getting your body ready makes the surgeon's work easier and your result more predictable.

Aesthetic surgery is a planned process. It should never be rushed. Every detail discussed during your consultation shapes the decisions made in the operating room, which is why real preparation starts weeks before the day itself.

What should you do 2 to 4 weeks before surgery?

This is the window for cleaning up and balancing your body. Quitting smoking, adjusting blood thinning medications and supplements with your doctor's approval, and moving to a balanced diet are the key points. Any requested lab tests should also be completed in this period.

  • Smoking and nicotine: They reduce blood flow to the tissue and clearly slow healing, so you are asked to stop at least 2 to 3 weeks before surgery.
  • Blood thinners: Aspirin, some painkillers and supplements such as vitamin E and fish oil can increase bleeding. Decide whether to pause them together with your doctor, never on your own.
  • Alcohol: It increases swelling and bleeding. Stop completely a few days before surgery.
  • Nutrition: A diet rich in protein, vitamin C and iron supports healing. Drink plenty of water.
  • Lab tests: Blood count and any other required tests are done during this time.

What should you do a few days before surgery?

The final days are about organisation. Clear your schedule for the first few days after surgery and set up a comfortable space to rest at home. Avoid anything that irritates your skin, sleep well, and follow your surgeon's fasting instructions exactly.

If general anaesthesia is planned, eating and drinking usually stop 6 to 8 hours before surgery. This can change depending on you and the procedure, so get the exact timing from your surgeon.

How should you prepare on the day of surgery?

Keep it simple that day. Wear loose, cotton clothing that buttons at the front, because raising your arms may be hard afterwards. Come without make up, nail polish or jewellery. Make sure someone is with you to take you home.

Leave valuables at home. Bringing a list of your regular medications helps the team look after you safely.

How do you prepare mentally?

Feeling excited and a little nervous is completely normal. The healthiest approach here is to set realistic expectations. Aesthetic surgery can help you feel fresher and more at ease with yourself, but it will not turn you into a different person. Results vary with each person's anatomy and healing.

Ask every question that is on your mind during the consultation. The more you talk, the calmer you will wake up.

What should you look for when choosing a surgeon?

Trust rests on the competence of the doctor doing the work. Specialisation in plastic, reconstructive and aesthetic surgery, national and international board qualifications, and a genuine follow up approach are the clearest signs you are in the right hands. Base your decision on the trust you build during the consultation, not on images alone.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long before surgery should I stop smoking?

The general recommendation is at least 2 to 3 weeks. Nicotine reduces blood flow to the tissue, which delays wound healing and raises the risk of scarring. The earlier you stop, the smoother your recovery.

Should I stop my medications?

Never stop them on your own. Some blood thinning medications and supplements can be adjusted, but only your doctor makes that decision. Bring a list of everything you take to your consultation.

Do I need to fast before surgery?

If general anaesthesia is planned, yes. Eating and drinking usually stop 6 to 8 hours beforehand. The exact time depends on the procedure and on you, so confirm it with your surgeon.

Do I need someone with me?

Yes, a companion is recommended for the day of surgery and the first day after. Driving or managing alone after anaesthesia is not safe.
